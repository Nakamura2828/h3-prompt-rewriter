THE STYLE VOCABULARY — THREE INDEPENDENT AXES
[[MEDIUM]] and [[SUB_MEDIUM]] answer HOW IT WAS MADE. [[IDIOM]] answers WHICH TRADITION IT DESCENDS FROM. [[TREATMENT]] answers WHICH COLOUR SYSTEM IT IS IN. These are independent: ANY idiom can appear in ANY medium. There is anime drawn in pencil, anime built from pixels, anime rendered in 3D. Choose each field on its own evidence. Never let one field decide another.

- [[MEDIUM]] MUST be exactly one of these eleven terms. No hedging, no qualifier, no combination of two.

  photograph — a camera image of real things, presented as a photograph rather than as a motion-picture frame
  live-action film — a frame from a live-action motion picture: film or digital-cinema grain, a graded look, cinematic lighting
  3D CG — a computer-generated three-dimensional rendering
  stop-motion — frame-by-frame photography of physical constructed puppets, models, or miniatures
  2D cel — FINISHED animation artwork: clean, evenly weighted outlines closing cleanly around areas of flat, production-clean colour, the way a frame of an animated film or series looks
  comic — a comic page or panel: panel borders, lettering, speech balloons, or comic-book inking
  painting — painted artwork with visible pigment handling, whether traditional or digital
  drawing — line-led artwork, where the drawn line carries the image and colour is secondary to it: varying or broken line weight, contours that do not close, construction lines, or colour blocked in under an obviously drawn structure
  vector — hard-edged flat geometric shapes with perfectly clean edges and no surface texture
  pixel art — artwork built from a visible grid of discrete pixels, usually on a limited palette
  print — a reproduction printing process: engraved lines, a halftone plate, a lithograph

- [[SUB_MEDIUM]] records WHAT THE MARKS READ AS BEING MADE WITH. It MUST be exactly one term from the sub-list belonging to the coarse term you chose:

  stop-motion — clay, puppet, figure, model
  2D cel — traditional cel, digital
  comic — ink, screentone, digital
  painting — oil, watercolour, digital
  drawing — marker, pencil, ink, digital
  print — engraving, halftone

- photograph, live-action film, 3D CG, vector and pixel art have NO sub-terms. For those five write "none". Never invent a sub-term, and never borrow one from another coarse term: "photograph / oil" and "painting / pencil" are both wrong.
- ALWAYS emit [[SUB_MEDIUM]]. Never leave it out or blank. "not determinable" is only for a genuine tie between two sub-terms of the same coarse term, never a substitute for "none" and never for a merely hard call — prefer committing to the closest term.
- Distinguishing the stop-motion terms: clay is modelled plasticine, matte and deformable with visible thumbprints; puppet is a fabricated character with fabric, fibre hair and sculpted painted surfaces; figure is a rigid moulded plastic toy with glossy surfaces, mould seams and joints; model is a built miniature object or set with no character in it.

- [[IDIOM]] MUST be exactly one of these five terms:

  anime — the Japanese animation and manga tradition
  western toon — the Western cartoon tradition: flat, boldly outlined, caricatured
  flat graphic — deliberate geometric vector-style illustration: forms simplified into clean flat planes and silhouettes, colour laid in as unbroken areas, form never rendered. This names a DESIGN APPROACH, not merely a simple or low-detail image. Anything that renders its subject the way it actually looks is "realist", however few colours it uses and however little detail it carries
  dimensional toon — caricatured, unrealistically proportioned character design rendered with REAL dimension, material and light rather than flat fills
  realist — aims at how things actually look, with no caricature and no stylistic tradition imposed

- ALWAYS emit [[IDIOM]]. "realist" is correct for an ordinary photograph, an ordinary film frame, and any artwork that simply depicts things as they look. It is a common answer, not a fallback to avoid.
- JUDGE [[IDIOM]] ON THE SUBJECT, NOT ON THE GROUND BEHIND IT. A plain white studio sweep, an empty colour backdrop, a blank margin, a printed border and an undecorated background are all just empty area. None of them is evidence of a flat graphic design approach. A subject rendered with real form and real material sitting on a perfectly flat ground is "realist". Ask how the SUBJECT is drawn, built or photographed, and ignore what is behind it.

- [[TREATMENT]] MUST be exactly one of these three terms:

  colour — ordinary full colour
  monochrome — black-and-white, greyscale, sepia, or any single-hue image, whether modern or aged
  vintage Technicolor — the saturated dye-transfer look of mid-century colour film

- ALWAYS emit [[TREATMENT]]. Spot colour on an otherwise black-and-white image is still "monochrome". An aged, faded or damaged photographic surface is still "monochrome" -- record the grain, fading and damage in [[EXECUTION]], never here.

TIE-BREAK RULES
These exist so that two records of one image cannot disagree while both being defensible.
1. STOP-MOTION BEATS PHOTOGRAPH. A stop-motion frame is literally a photograph of physical objects. When what is photographed is a constructed puppet, model or miniature, write "stop-motion", never "photograph".
2. REPORT THE OUTER MEDIUM. When the image contains another image — a painting held in a hand, a poster on a wall, a picture on a television or phone screen — record the medium of the OUTER image, the one the camera made. A photograph of a watercolour is "photograph", not "painting".
3. FLAT BEATS DIMENSIONAL ON [[IDIOM]]. "anime" and "western toon" are for flat, outline-bounded, non-dimensional rendering. When a caricatured, unrealistically proportioned character is rendered with real volume, real material and real light, that is "dimensional toon", whatever the medium. A puppet built in that design language and a digital 3D render in that design language are the same idiom.
4. JUDGE ON PRESENTATION, NOT PROVENANCE — ON EVERY AXIS. Judge each field on what the image looks like, never on how you suppose the file was really produced. Grain, a graded palette, shaped cinematic lighting and aspect-ratio bars point to "live-action film"; a clean, evenly lit, ungraded camera image is "photograph". The same rule governs [[SUB_MEDIUM]]: write "digital" only when the marks THEMSELVES read as digital, with no medium physics, no wet edges, no visible tooth or weave, uniform fills and even line weight. An image that convincingly imitates a physical medium takes that medium's term — "painting / oil" for marks that behave like oil paint, whatever made them.
5. VISIBLE PROCESS MEANS DRAWING, NOT 2D CEL. Flat colour alone does not make an image animation artwork. Ask whether the linework is finished and production-clean, closing cleanly the way an animation cel does, or whether the act of drawing is still on the surface — construction lines left in, contours that do not close, line weight that varies stroke to stroke. Where the drawing process is still visible, write "drawing", however flat the colour sitting under it, and however strongly the character design reads as anime or cartoon. The tradition belongs in [[IDIOM]]; it is not evidence about [[MEDIUM]].
